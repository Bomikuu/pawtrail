# Maya mismatch diagnosis

The original 96×96 illustration is authoritative. At 192×192 it is enlarged exactly 2× with nearest sampling. Reference landmarks were manually annotated on the original; the partly hair-covered left brow is less certain. The 1–2 native-pixel target is a calibration goal, not artistic acceptance.

- Hair: the drawing has a distinct off-center side part, a wide swept fringe, a low rounded crown, asymmetric masses and outward bob tips close to the jaw/neck. The procedural bust has a taller cap, repeated sweeping ridges, more symmetrical sides and long locks over the chest. More hair texture repeats the wrong masses.
- Face: the drawing is already turned and tilted; its visible cheek and tapered chin are asymmetric. The prototype uses a mostly symmetric front-facing lathed head with a broader, younger-looking face.
- Eyes: the source eyes differ in apparent size and angle, have specific upper-lid contours and small painted highlights. The prototype's evenly spaced, near-circular eyes and generic brows change her identity.
- Nose/mouth: the original includes subtle painted nose marks and an off-center, rising smile. The prototype deliberately omitted the nose under an older preference and centers a generic mouth. For Maya's new reference-specific asset, the supplied illustration now takes precedence; generic gameplay faces remain unchanged.
- Skin: the reference has localized warm ochre/red shadow clusters beneath hair and around cheek/jaw. The prototype's broad beige surface and generic blush placement flatten those planes.
- Bust: the original has a short neck, angled shoulders, an open green utility vest with specific lapels, ochre sleeves and a low neckline. The prototype uses a straighter neck/shoulder silhouette and a central cream shirt.
- Camera: turning a generic symmetrical model by an arbitrary yaw is not reference calibration. The new comparison pins a single orthographic camera/pose and never auto-fits candidates independently.

Only the reference angle is evidenced. The unseen profile and back require deliberate artist interpretation and additional references if exact identity is expected from those angles.
