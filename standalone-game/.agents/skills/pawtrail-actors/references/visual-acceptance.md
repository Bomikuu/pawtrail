# Live visual acceptance for each actor

Apply the early face/native-icon checks in
[production efficiency](production-efficiency.md) before expanding those assets.
They reduce late rework; every new actor still needs the full applicable final
review below. Reuse prior observations only for exactly unchanged review units
and dependencies, never as acceptance for a new character or unreviewed state.

The owner requests browser visual review for every new actor and relevant actor
revision. Installation is not acceptance. Open the actual actor preview in Firefox
when supported; explain a tool limitation before choosing another browser. Honor
any later explicit restriction and record a genuinely unavailable review as
pending, never passed. This authorizes focused visual interaction, not unrelated
application tests, builds, live-roster promotion or external writes.

Compare the actor with its own selected illustration and portrait. Match hair
parting/accent placement, eye color, face shape, cape length/attachment, shoulder
armor, clothing layers, weapon and palette. Check front, three-quarter, side and
back at ordinary display size and enlarged inspection. A loaded, error-free model
can still fail likeness or anatomy. Repair visible mismatches before handoff.

The approved animated illustration is the model's primary design anchor; a later
Refined cel portrait may deliberately have a different rendering style. Compare
model geometry/materials against the illustration, and identity against both.
When needed, generate static matching turnaround views before model construction
to resolve side/back shapes without inventing inconsistent costume details.
Default portrait and compact-icon framing to three-quarter; confirm angle choices
still work and the selected portrait angle reaches the dialogue. Inspect the
illustration's semi-bold outer contour at normal size and throughout the loop;
finer facial lines should not disappear into a uniformly heavy border.
Default illustration style is **Refined cel + painterly**, combining new
Sieghart's mature painted detail/materials with old Sieg's deliberate pixel/cel
structure and semi-bold edges. Compare at equal head sizes against **both**
references; an entirely old-style replacement or pure new painting misses the brief.
Compare icons against the
approved normal illustration, not a separately chosen dialogue portrait.
For enrage, inspect the owner's demonic smiling/angry reference and the actor's
own corresponding expression/attack pose. Review multiple complete motion cycles:
face/body/grip stay anchored while hair and garments follow through naturally.
Reject pose popping, sparse long holds that expose jumps, mechanical reversals,
rigid cloth boards, changing face proportions and loop seams. Sieghart v2's
previous acceptance record does not override the owner's newer motion rejection.
Sieghart's initial v3 part rig was also too subtle for the owner at preview size;
its earlier review is not evidence that imperceptible motion is acceptable.
Hide the background and aura and confirm that the actor's own hair tips and cape/
scarf/robe hems visibly move at the normal destination size. Tune weighted tip
travel to that silhouette and scale: subpixel hair movement and barely moving
cloth can read as a still despite a live timer. Restore the effects and check
readability again. Capture several phases across complete loops and inspect actual
attachment/overlap changes; different hashes or status text alone do not pass.
Check that preview state updates do not restart playback, and review pause,
restart, visibility return and the reduced-motion still. For supernatural enrage,
check the separate facial effect's attachment and animation without distorting the
grin/glare. Hair must visibly loop throughout active playback in both variants;
do not accept moving cloth/aura with stationary hair. Where lightning is present,
isolate it and inspect moving arc paths, traveling branch energy or moving tips;
a fixed bolt that only brightens/dims fails this check. Preserve intentional pause,
reduced motion and offscreen suspension. Match the three-quarter icon to the approved normal illustration.
Observe a complete authored illustration blink in each variant where eyes are visible,
with steady brows, jaw, hair and expression. Hair/robe/garment travel must meet the
owner's moderately stronger motion preference; imperceptible movement fails even
with an active timer. Sparse captures that miss a blink leave that check pending.
Inspect the detailed glowing ground circle while idle, moving and floating:
glyphs stay readable, the circle stays on the floor, and the contact shadow remains
separate. A cosmetic circle must not imply damage or a confirmed action outcome.

Make this a side-by-side likeness pass in the live preview for every new or
revised model. The illustration is the shape/material reference, not merely a
palette to apply to a generic body. Compare the visible face/jaw, hair parting and
tuft silhouette, shoulders, waist, limb thickness, coat length/splits, accessories
and weapon from front, three-quarter, side and back. Check at comparable body/head
sizes. Record specific differences and fix them; loading successfully or sharing
a skeleton does not establish a match.

Use layered painted textures on appropriate three-dimensional forms: curved
skull/cheeks, shaped hair masses with overlapping locks and tapered tips, rounded
or tapered shoulders/limbs, separate raised collars/lapels/armor edges and garment
shells/linings/tails. Give the side view real depth. A curved contour drawn on a
flat box does not round the silhouette, and texture detail cannot replace missing
geometry or clothing layers. Preserve reference-specific angular armor where
intended. Retain the common skeleton, animation pivots and world scale; review
contacts, collisions/clipping and cloth roots after shape changes.

Always include every applicable long hair/robe/cape/scarf/ribbon in that review.
Record its textured front, reverse/lining and edge treatment, side-view volume
and full swing clearance. Look for blank backs, stretched UVs, disappearing faces,
flat hair sheets and rigid board-like garments in idle, walking, running and
applicable actions. Use shaped shells/folds and layered hair masses while keeping
the shared rig; a double-sided plane alone does not establish finished volume.

Fit face UV artwork to the curved face's feature coordinates; do not stretch an
entire illustrated bust, hair or collar over the head. Separate hair geometry and
its maps from the face/eyes, and verify all authored open/half/closed maps on the
actual mesh. Keep fronts and backs distinct: e.g. a white vest front must not
appear on a dark coat back because a front-projected texture was wrapped around
the entire torso. Scarf and cloth layers follow the reference's attachment,
length, curves and wind direction, rather than defaulting to symmetric straight
boards on both shoulders. Shape changes still share the existing action rig.

Use the real controls to observe idle, walking, running and the entire supported
action set. Inspect more than one moment per action. Include feeding, petting,
water, pickup/cleaning, sitting, sleeping and expressive actions. Check feet,
hand contacts, prop visibility/holstering, garment/armor clearance and a clean
return to idle. Sample walking during layered actions and pause/resume. Hair tips
and cloth hems should lag from stable roots without clipping, whole-body jitter,
phase resets or detached ornaments. Follow the existing action-review contract.

Check the portrait alongside the body: each intended emotion, selected angle,
authored blink/speech states, breathing and dialogue transitions must use that
character's assets. Inspect every angle/emotion against its source drawings;
passing a neutral rest does not pass talking or smiling. Check identity and facial
readability at the actual compact icon, dialogue and creator sizes. Measure tight
visible content bounds across all complete frames. Blank-cell dimensions and
centered bounding boxes can leave a tiny face or crop the chin; fix registration
and framing without inventing runtime face patches. Keep icons compact and use
the existing wood frame/active glow.
Explicitly exercise Speaking preview and dialogue text reveal even without voices.
Observe actual authored mouth-shape changes and the return to the correct rest in
every requested angle/emotion, alongside compatible blinking and breathing. A pressed
speaking control, true flag or unused speech artwork does not pass.

Keep the actor dominant while making the context background and aura clearly
recognizable at normal size. Inspect defining scene details and the aura's shape,
color and moving energy independently, then together. Neither may disappear into
an indistinct dark wash or barely visible glow. Each toggle must visibly change
the composition without obscuring the face/costume. Validate reduced-motion stills too.
Inspect normal and enraged animated illustrations: the latter must change the
body pose/gesture to attack mode while retaining identity, style, outlines and
natural secondary motion. Verify their selector, playback and return behavior
when installed. Record the illustration feedback separately: explicit response,
requested corrections, or continuation without feedback under the owner's rule.
Check the actor's magic circle on the ground below the feet in idle and movement,
including a floating/jumping action: it follows the ground anchor, remains distinct
from the shadow and damaging telegraphs, and respects pause/reduced motion/exit.

For an adult brief, check adult proportions in the illustration before model
authoring: restrained head/eye size, mature face construction, suitable shoulder
width and torso/leg length. Adult fantasy references often use roughly 7–8 heads
in full-body illustration; use the actual reference, not a universal ratio. Keep
the selected pixel style, costume and identity. The small gameplay rig may retain
stylized world proportions; do not silently turn all existing actors into taller
models. Childlike/chibi proportions are deliberate only when requested.

Record observed actions/angles/emotions, screenshots or short clips, findings,
repairs, remaining limitations and browser console failures. Use visual evidence
to approve appearance and motion; state logs, changing frame hashes and absence
of errors alone are insufficient. Repeat affected checks after repairs. Preserve
the latest voice omission or other explicitly excluded stages.
