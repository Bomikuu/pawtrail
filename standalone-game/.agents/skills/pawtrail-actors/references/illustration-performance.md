# Illustration and portrait performance

Applies to future creation and explicitly requested revisions. Do not retroactively
regenerate Rika or other actors for a skill-only update. Preserve the selected style,
separate layer assets, 300 KB portrait budget, original sources and full quality matrix.

## Clear background and aura

At the actual preview/loading-screen size, the enabled background must communicate
its setting through recognizable shapes and details. The aura must visibly accent
the silhouette with its own shape, color and animated energy/motes. Inspect each
layer alone and together; turning either off must produce an obvious difference.
Do not reuse an older pack's very low opacity values without judging the result.
Keep the character dominant through composition, local contrast and selective
detail, rather than globally darkening/fading the scene and energy. Avoid muddy
backdrops, washed-out effects, face-covering glow, excessive clutter and strobing.
Retain independent controls and a readable reduced-motion composition.

## More visible secondary motion

Use stronger hair/robe/cape/ribbon/loose-garment movement than the previous subtle
preset, with a slightly quicker secondary-motion cycle. Increase actual tip/hem
travel, not just playback speed: it must be obvious at normal preview size with
background and aura disabled. As a starting point for a previously too-subtle
performance, try roughly 1.5–2× the loose-tip travel and a 10–20% shorter sway
cycle, then adapt to the character and material. These are tuning guides, not
fixed requirements for every silhouette. Keep roots, seams,
feet, grip and face shape anchored. Weight displacement toward tips; stagger strands,
folds and trailing layers with smooth easing and follow-through. Repair underlays
and check maximum swing, outlines, overlaps and loop seams. Do not move the whole
image, shake the head or bend rigid armor to manufacture more motion.

Keep body breathing calm and independent of the quicker loose-part sway. Add
enough visible in-betweens for the increased travel; preserve the renderer's
frame limits, texture budget and sharp outlines. Reuse approved source art and
version the motion pack/binding so the preview cannot serve a cached old loop.

## Illustration blinking

Both normal and enraged illustrations include natural blinks when eyes are visible,
while holding their own expression. Author matching open, half-closed, closed and
reopening drawings in that illustration's style, pose, lighting and registration.
A blink must visibly reach the closed-eye drawing in the actual actor layer;
metadata or an aura flash does not count. A roughly 100–180 ms closed phase with
brief half-closed lead-in/reopening is a useful starting point, tuned to the art.
A brief blink roughly every 3–6 seconds is a starting point; vary timing naturally
rather than synchronizing every blink to the sway loop. Preserve brows, jaw and
expression; do not borrow portrait eyes or draw generic procedural eyelids. Already
closed eyes and opaque eye coverings retain their intentional appearance.
Use a real authored blink track/complete frames supported by shared playback.
If the contract lacks that binding, add it within the authorized implementation;
unused drawings or ignored timing fields are incomplete. Observe complete blinks
in both variants alongside hair/garment motion, pause/resume and reduced motion.

## Portrait talking, including voiceless actors

Omitting voices does not omit visible talking. Drive the character's own authored
whole portrait speech frames from Speaking preview and dialogue text reveal, not
only from an audio file. In every requested angle/emotion, observe resting, small
and open mouth shapes changing, compatible blinking/breathing, and return to the
correct rest after text, cancellation or dialogue close. Retain the emotion's brows,
lip corners and face registration. Never reuse one neutral mouth across all emotions
or procedurally assemble the face. A speaking flag/button, generated mouth sheet or
successful asset load with no visible mouth motion fails acceptance.

Record these checks in the job's review evidence before approval. If a capture
misses a blink or speech phase, inspect it live or capture again; hashes and timers
cannot substitute for observation. Preserve the full required matrix and existing
restrictions on agents/tests/browser work. A skill-only revision generates no new
assets and does not count as a runtime fix.
