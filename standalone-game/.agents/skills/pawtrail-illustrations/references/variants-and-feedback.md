# Normal and enraged illustrations, then user feedback

## Two authored performances

Full new-actor illustration delivery includes a normal living illustration and
an **enraged / attack-mode illustration** unless the owner narrows the scope.
The latter is a distinct illustrated performance: change the body pose, gesture,
expression and applicable weapon grip to an attack-ready or attacking silhouette.
A red tint, enlarged aura, emoji or angry face on the unchanged normal pose does
not satisfy it. Preserve the same identity, costume, anatomy, selected style and
semi-bold silhouette contour. Each variant holds its own expression while hair,
robe/cape and appropriate loose details move naturally; this is not a dialogue
emotion/blink/speech pack.

## Enraged expression reference

Inspect the owner's [demonic smiling/angry reference](../assets/enraged-demonic-expression-reference.png)
before choosing the expression. Its useful signals are sharply angled brows,
narrow/slanted eyes, a forward-inclined head and a dangerous, slightly asymmetric
grin with readable upper teeth. Carry that menacing battle confidence into the
actor's own face. An angry performance can instead use an intense glare, tense
mouth and compressed brows. Choose the version that fits the actor/brief; avoid
a mild ordinary smile, an unchanged neutral face or a generic angry emoji.

Retain adult construction and the selected **Refined cel + painterly** blend:
new Sieghart's painted depth/detail with old Sieg's pixel/cel accents and edges.
The supplied character is youthful/stylized: it supplies expression and gesture,
not a mandate for its proportions, identity, costume, weapon, horns or fangs.
Eye glow or demonic aura can support the face as separate effects where fitting,
without hiding the eyes/mouth. Avoid accidental facial distortion or a comical
bug-eyed grin. The earlier subtle Sieghart v2 smirk is not the expression anchor
for this new direction.

## Natural motion is a required gate

The owner rejected the recent Sieghart v2 illustration movement as awkward.
Its old reviewed label does not approve that motion for future work. Preserve
good identity/style sources, but diagnose pose jumps, torso drift, cloth roots,
hand/weapon contact and first/last continuity before reusing a loop.

Keep the attack-ready body's anatomy and weapon grip anchored. Animate cloth
and hair from their actual roots with lag, staggered peaks and an eased return;
the head and grin should not wobble or change shape between frames. Match wind,
weight, folds, overlaps and direction through the loop. A tense held attack pose
needs controlled secondary motion, not repeated lunges, a swinging cardboard
cape or whole-body scaling. See [motion construction](motion-and-export.md#loop-construction)
for in-between production, timing and the existing complete-frame playback path.

Watch multiple full cycles at intended size and inspect the seam, rest points,
grip and cloth overlap. Static sheets, frame-count metadata and different hashes
do not establish natural movement. A failed motion review blocks dependent
acceptance; the optional no-response feedback rule does not waive that gate.

## Variant packaging

Keep each actor variant, poster and motion metadata separately addressable.
Context background and aura remain independent layers: reuse a suitable backdrop,
and author an intensified aura only where the new performance calls for one.
Check safe bounds and stable anatomy anchors for the changed pose rather than
stretching the normal pose's bounds. Record normal/enraged runtime bytes in the
illustration bundle, separately from the dialogue portrait's 300 KB allowance.

In an authorized preview installation, let the user select normal/enraged and
inspect both actual loops. Gameplay cut-ins, when requested, follow real accepted
action/state entry, interruption and exit, restoring the normal presentation.
The artwork alone does not apply damage or turn an actor into a boss. A boss's
centered phase-enrage cut-in retains the separate encounter-state contract in
[actor feedback](../../pawtrail-actors/references/feedback-and-effects.md).

## Show the result and ask

After authoring and the applicable technical/visual review, show the actual
normal and enraged loops or a playable preview link; include their stills when
inline animation cannot be shown. Ask a concise feedback question through the
available asynchronous user-input tool, for example: “Is this illustration
direction good, or should I adjust it?” Suggested answers can be “Looks good”
and “Request adjustments.” The question is feedback on work already authorized.

The owner explicitly permits continuation without a reply to this particular
illustration checkpoint. Give a reasonable opportunity to respond (normally
60 seconds), doing independent useful work meanwhile. If no reply arrives,
continue the existing version under that standing instruction and record
**continued without feedback**, not **user approved**. Do not end the task solely
to await this optional feedback. A rejection or requested change overrides the
no-response continuation: incorporate the correction before building dependent
assets. Reconcile later feedback with work already in progress.

This continuation rule does not waive failed visual checks, supply a missing
portrait-style choice, or authorize publishing, paid services, roster promotion
or unrelated external actions. Preserve a selected portrait style already given
for the current job; otherwise ask that separate style question as usual.
